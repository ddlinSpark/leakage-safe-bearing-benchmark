# Validation-to-Test Domain Gap

## Definition

For each run:

```text
absolute gap = validation Macro-F1 - test Macro-F1
relative drop = 100 * absolute gap / validation Macro-F1
```

Each summary is the mean and sample standard deviation over 20 fold-seed runs.
Validation Macro-F1 is the best validation Macro-F1 used for early stopping.
Test Macro-F1 is computed after fitting on the held-out bearing codes and test
condition. No test metric is used for early stopping or model selection.

Source:

- `results/raw_runs.csv`, columns `best_val_macro_f1` and `macro_f1`.
- `results/domain_gap_summary.csv`, automatically generated by
  `src/spindle_cnn/analysis/statistics.py`.

## Results

| Model | Validation Macro-F1 | Test Macro-F1 | Absolute gap | Relative drop | Runs |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Raw 1D-CNN | 0.853 ± 0.153 | 0.289 ± 0.104 | 0.564 | 66.4% | 20 |
| CWT 2D-CNN | 0.808 ± 0.112 | 0.291 ± 0.086 | 0.516 | 62.4% | 20 |
| CWT-GRU | 0.797 ± 0.115 | 0.280 ± 0.107 | 0.518 | 63.0% | 20 |
| CWT-GRU without SE | 0.809 ± 0.139 | 0.307 ± 0.103 | 0.503 | 59.6% | 20 |

The absolute gap ranges from 0.503 to 0.564 Macro-F1. The proportional
validation-to-test drop ranges from 59.6% to 66.4%.

## Interpretation

The substantially higher validation Macro-F1 suggests that the low held-out test
scores cannot be explained merely by a failure to fit the development domains.
The performance loss coincides with the simultaneous change to unseen bearing
identities and an unseen operating condition.

This observed pattern is consistent with the intended joint domain shift. The
design cannot isolate the causal contribution of bearing identity, operating
condition, damage origin, and their interaction. Those factors are deliberately
held out together, so the current experiment cannot estimate their individual
effects.

The result also argues against selecting a method from validation performance
alone. Validation-to-test gaps must be shown for each method and fold.

## Signal-level diagnostics

The vibration channel changes at the domain level in RMS, kurtosis, crest
factor, spectral centroid, and spectral entropy. The standardized train-to-test
differences vary in sign and magnitude across the four condition folds. These
statistics describe the joint shift; they do not identify which factor caused
the lower test score.

## Label boundary

Post-hoc coarsening of the same fitted outputs shows that healthy-versus-fault
Macro-F1 is higher than four-class Macro-F1 for the principal models. A
substantial part of the error therefore concerns distinction among fault
locations and multiple damage rather than complete loss of anomaly detection.
This analysis does not change the primary four-class task.

## Confounding boundary

All four held-out fault codes have real lifetime damage. The fitting set mixes
artificial and real damage for outer- and inner-ring faults and contains only
real-damage multiple-damage bearings. Bearing identity, condition, and damage
origin therefore change together. The correct interpretation is a coincident
joint shift, not a causal attribution to operating condition.
